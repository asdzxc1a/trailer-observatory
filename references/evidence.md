# Evidence semantics and quality limits

Each requested source second `n` denotes the bin `[n, n+1)`. Its evidence sample targets `n+0.5` seconds. Accurate ffmpeg seeking and timestamp-aware selection choose the first decoded source frame at or after that target. `showinfo` records its actual presentation timestamp in `decoded_frame_time_seconds`; the target and decoded time remain separate. Precision is limited by the source frame rate. This is a still-frame observation, not a claim that the worker watched every moving frame or heard the soundtrack.

The worker remotely probes decoded frame timestamps and the final frame duration to establish video EOF. Displayed YouTube integer duration can round up. A final bin containing real video but shorter than one second is labeled `partial_bin: true`, with its actual exclusive end. Its target is the midpoint of available coverage, capped at the nominal midpoint. It is separately extracted rather than duplicated/padded. A wholly absent bin fails; a partial bin without a decoded evidence frame also fails. Where a source omits final-frame duration, the average-frame-rate duration fallback is explicitly recorded in `decoded_eof_precision`.

Every successful record contains its public source URL and video ID, integer second, midpoint sample target, decoded image path and SHA-256, perceptual hash, source metadata and downloaded-file SHA-256, pinned model ID/revision, worker version, caption/review status, and separated `observation`/`inference` fields. Run provenance stores source commit, package versions, ffmpeg, timing and model prompt. A reviewer can correct a caption while retaining provenance and set `annotation_status` to `human_reviewed` and `human_reviewed` to true. Store reviewer identity and review time as additional fields if useful.

SmolVLM-256M is a small, free, Apache-2.0 open model, selected for CPU throughput. Its captions are `machine_unreviewed`; they can misidentify characters, expressions, object counts or text. A draft is not authoritative. Exact repeated frames can reuse a caption, but every bin retains its own timestamp and image. pHash groups candidate duplicates; only exact SHA matches permit reuse. Near matches receive individual inference to retain small acting details.

Missing frames, missing/empty captions, wrong source IDs, wrong midpoint timestamps, mutated images, duplicated bins and download failures never count as completed documented observations. `audit.json` reports expected bins, successful evidence-backed rows, failed bins, missing bins, validation errors and completion separately. A declaration that asks for 1,561 rows does not mean 1,561 have been produced or reviewed.

`cut_candidate` is a 1 fps proxy combining pHash distance (at least 18 bits) and normalized RGB change (at least 0.18) between successive samples. It may mistake a flash, pan or large subject motion for a cut and misses subsecond edits. Its cut rate, median sampled segment and quarter-wise rates support pacing comparison; they are not precise shot counts. No audio/dialogue/acting judgments are derived from these proxies.

A creative comparison must examine original images and the actual edited trailer with an independent judge. Use separate criteria for premise clarity, character specificity, conflict, escalation, staging, expressions, continuity, animation quality, sound and music. Record evidence for a defect and a revision. Similar structural numbers never prove equal feature-animation quality; a 90-second animatic and a finished Disney/Pixar trailer have different production maturity.
